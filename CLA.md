Thank you for contributing. This agreement records who owns the copyright in what you contribute,

You keep the right to use your own contribution for anything you like: see section 4.

## 1. Definitions

**"Contribution"** means any original work of authorship — code, documentation, configuration,
tests, artwork, translations — that you intentionally submit to the project, in any form and
through any channel, including pull requests, patches, issues and discussion posts.

**"You"** means the individual signing, or the legal entity on whose behalf it is signed. An entity
and its affiliates are treated as a single signatory.

## 2. Assignment of copyright

You assign to the Owner all right, title and interest in the copyright in your Contributions,
worldwide and for the full duration of those rights, including all renewals and extensions.

To the extent any moral or similar right cannot be assigned, you agree not to assert it against the
Owner or anyone receiving the work from the Owner, so far as the law where you live allows.

## 3. Fallback license, if assignment does not take effect

Assignment of copyright is not fully effective in every country. Where it is not — in whole or in
part, for any reason — you instead grant the Owner a **perpetual, worldwide, exclusive,
irrevocable, transferable, sublicensable, royalty-free license** to use, reproduce, modify,
distribute, publicly perform and display, and create derivative works from your Contributions, and
to license and relicense them on any terms, including proprietary terms.

This section exists so that the Owner's position is the same everywhere, whatever the local law on
assignment says.

## 4. What you keep

You retain the right to use, publish and license your own Contributions for any purpose, without
restriction and without needing the Owner's permission. Assigning copyright to the Owner does not
take your own work away from you; it means the Owner also owns it and does not need to ask you
about it.

## 5. Patents

You grant the Owner and every recipient of the project a perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable,
royalty-free license under any patent claims you own or control that are necessarily infringed by
your Contribution alone or by its combination with the project, to make, have made, use, sell,
offer to sell, import and otherwise transfer the project.

This grant terminates for anyone who brings a patent action alleging that the project or a
Contribution infringes a patent.

## 6. What you are promising

You confirm that:

- Each Contribution is your own original work, or you have the right to submit it and have
  identified any part that is not — including its source and its license.
- Your Contribution does not knowingly infringe anyone's copyright, patent, trade secret or other
  right.
- If your employer has rights in work you produce, either you have their permission to contribute,
  or they have waived those rights, or an authorised person is signing this on the employer's
  behalf.
- If you are signing for an entity, you are authorised to do so.

## 7. No obligation, and no warranty

The Owner is not required to use, merge or keep any Contribution. Except as stated in section 6,
Contributions are provided as-is, without warranty of any kind.

## How to sign

Contributions are checked automatically. The first time you open a pull request, a bot will comment
with a link and ask you to confirm that you have read and agree to this document; signing takes one
click and is recorded against your account for future pull requests.

## Why an assignment rather than a licence-back

Most projects use a CLA, where the contributor keeps copyright and grants the project a broad
licence. This project uses assignment because the Owner intends to keep the option of licensing
the codebase on different terms in future — a proprietary or dual-licensed edition, or a move to a
more permissive licence. Under a plain inbound-equals-outbound arrangement, that decision would
require the agreement of every contributor who ever landed a patch, which in practice means it can
never be made.

If you would rather not assign copyright, you are still very welcome to open issues, report bugs
and discuss designs; those are not Contributions under section 1 unless they contain code or text
intended for the project.
